# Memo: We're Losing Almost Every Customer After Their First Order

**To:** CEO

**From:** Data Analytics

**Date:** August 2026

**Re:** What our order data says about customer retention

## The short version

We looked at two years of order data, from September 2016 to August 2018, and found a clear problem: **most customers never come back after their first purchase**.

Out of around 96,096 customers, only **3.04% placed a second order**. This pattern is consistent across different months, so it doesn't seem to be caused by one unusually bad period.

We're bringing new customers in, but we're having a hard time getting them to return.

## What we found

**Only about 3.04% of customers come back.**

We looked at customers based on the month they made their first purchase. Across most groups, the pattern is very similar: only a small number of customers make another purchase. This suggests that low repeat purchases are a broader business issue, rather than something limited to a particular month.

**Most customers who don't come back leave early.**

The biggest drop happens right after the first order. This makes the period shortly after the first purchase an important time to focus on getting customers to come back.

**Revenue is growing, but we rely heavily on new customers.**

Total revenue reached **R$15.74M**, with an average order value of around **R$160.27**. The revenue trend looks healthy, but the low repeat purchase rate means much of this revenue depends on continually bringing in new customers.

If customers don't return, we have to keep finding new customers to maintain growth. Improving repeat purchases could help us get more value from the customers we already have.

**São Paulo is a good place to start.**

São Paulo generates about a third of our total revenue, making it our largest market. Since we already have a large number of customers there, we can test a retention strategy in São Paulo first and see if it helps bring more customers back.

## What we'd suggest doing

1. **Start with a small test.**  
   Send first-time customers a follow-up email, discount code, or reminder around 2–3 weeks after their first purchase. Start with customers in São Paulo and see whether this increases repeat purchases before expanding the approach.

2. **Find out why customers don't return.**  
   Look at a sample of one-time customers and compare factors such as delivery time, review scores, product categories, and order experience. This could help us understand what is stopping customers from buying again.

3. **Track repeat purchases regularly.**  
   Add repeat purchase rate to our regular business metrics, alongside revenue and order volume. This will help us see whether customer retention is improving over time.

## A quick note on the numbers

The analysis covers **September 2016 through August 2018**. We excluded September 2018 because it was only a partial month in the data. Early cohorts (September–December 2016) also have very few customers and were excluded from the retention trend analysis.

The **3.04% repeat purchase rate is low**, and the fact that we see a similar pattern across different cohorts makes it worth investigating further. The data doesn't tell us exactly why customers aren't returning, but it does show that retention is an important area to focus on.
